\section{Adenoviruses}
Adenoviruses are among the largest DNA viruses \cite{heimAdenoviren2016a}. They were first discovered in adenoid tissue cultures undergoing spontaneous degeneration, hence the name \cite{roweIsolationCytopathogenicAgent1953}. Collectively, they infect most species \cite{harrachAdenovirusesAnimalKingdom2019}. \acp{hadv} mostly cause mild upper respiratory or gastrointestinal disease. A notable exeption is the epidemic keratoconjuntivitis caused by \ac{hadv-d} \cite{pettitChronicKeratoconjunctivitisAssociated1979}. Severe disease is possible in immunocompetent hosts, albeit uncommon. \cite{parkThinsectionComputedTomography2017}. Immunocompromised hosts, such as organ transplat reciepients, have increased risk for severe disease. Clinical manifestations in this group are broad and include pneumonia, hepatitis and meningitis \cite{ljungmanRespiratoryVirusInfection1989}. 
\\ 
The repertoire of adenovirus types is vast. To date, there are 118 known subtypes across seven \acs{hadv} species \cite{dadkhahEvolutionHumanAdenoviruses2026}. The latest, \acs{hadv-d}-118, was added in august 2026 \cite{HAdVWorkingGroupb}. \ac{ad5} is the most studied and widely used adenovirus in current clinical applications \cite{salauddinClinicalApplicationAdenovirus2024}.

\section{Human Adenovirus Type 5}

\ac{ad5} is a non-enveloped, \ac{dsdna} virus. The virus capsid measures $\sim$95 nm and has an icosaedric shape \cite{fabryQuasiatomicModelHuman2005}. The most abundant strucural element of the capsid are 240 hexon protein trimeres. At the vertices of the virion sit 12 penton pentameres, each anchoring a fiber multimere protruding outwards from the base \cite{liuModelTrimericFiber2011}. Mutliple smaller proteins aid in stabilizing the capsid and localizing the genome to the structural multimeres.

\section{Viral Vectors in Emerging Therapies}

Clinical medicine progressively transitioning from broad, non-specific treatments such as chemotherapy or immunosuppressants to more refined, high-fidelity solutions. Those include small molecules targeting pathways of the immune system, antibody treatments, and more recently, gene therapy and virotherapy. Virotherapy emerges as a potentially promising approach for treating various cancers \cite{xiaoOncolyticVirusesAdvanced2026}, while gene therapy shows promise for hereditary diseases \cite{shchaslyvyiCurrentStateHuman2023}, and genetic vaccination is being explored for the treatment and prevention of infectious diseases \cite{zhouDNABasedVaccinesAdvances2026}. Gene therapy utilizes vectors to deliver genetic material to the target cell. Numerous vector systems are currently in use and are broadly split into viral and non-viral vectors. Non-viral vectors are further categorized into physical and chemical vectors. Viral vectors are particularly favored due to their evolutionary adaptation for efficient cell transduction as intracellular parasites \cite{kajanVirusHostCoevolution2020}. The most prevalent viral vectors employed in clinical trials are adenoviral vectors, followed by adeno-associated virus and lentiviral vectors \cite{bulchaViralVectorPlatforms2021}.

\section{CRISPR Sequences and Cas Proteins}
The CRISPR-Cas system can be described in two ways: originally, it is a bacterial defence component against foreign genetic material. After its discovery, it has been studied and employed to induce precise strand breaks in DNA, thereby facilitating genetic engineering. Its components include the growing family of \ac{cas} nucleases, which perform the strand cleavage, and the \ac{crispr} sequences. Long before the role of this system was understood, repetitive interspersed sequences were discovered in \textit{Escherichia coli} \cite{ishinoNucleotideSequenceIap1987}. The discovery of similar sequences in archaea sparked new interest in the topic, as the presence of similar sequences in phylogenetically distant organisms implies a high level of evolutionary conservation and, consequently, significant biological importance \cite{Si2005}. It was later shown that the highly variable spacer sequences represent genetic information of foreign, viral origin \cite{pourcelCRISPRElementsYersinia2005,bolotinClusteredRegularlyInterspaced2005} and are accompanied by associated Cas genes \cite{jansenIdentificationGenesThat2002}. These genes maintain and expand the \ac{crispr} loci (class I) or act as direct effector molecules in the cleavage of foreign DNA or RNA (class II) \cite{shmakovSystematicPredictionGenes2018}. At present, more than 30 different \ac{cas} genes in both classes and numerous subtypes have been described \cite{makarovaEvolutionaryClassificationCRISPR2020}. Other essential components are the \ac{crrna} and the \ac{tracrrna}. The \ac{crrna} is complementary to the targeted foreign DNA or RNA (the protospacer), is derived from the spacer region of the CRISPR locus, and has a length of approximately 20 bp \cite{brounsSmallCRISPRRNAs2008}. The \ac{tracrrna} is important for the maturation of the \ac{crrna} and for assembly of the final functional effector ribonucleoprotein complex consisting of the \ac{cas} protein and associated RNAs \cite{deltchevaCRISPRRNAMaturation2011}. Additionally, the protospacer must be adjacent to a \ac{pam}, whose sequence varies depending on the Cas type and class. The \acp{pam} are not integrated into the spacer sequences but are recognised by the Cas proteins. For example, Cas9 from \textit{Streptococcus pyogenes} requires an NGG PAM sequence directly downstream of the crRNA binding site and cleaves three base pairs upstream of the PAM. Because crRNA and tracrRNA are separate molecules, they were subsequently combined into a single \ac{sgrna} to facilitate application of the system \textit{in vitro} \cite{jinekProgrammableDualRNAguidedDNA2012}.

To apply the system to edit eukaryotic DNA, the Cas9 sequence is codon-optimised and paired with a nuclear localisation signal \cite{maliRNAGuidedHumanGenome2013}. For our application, genetically modifying HEK293 cells, we chose Cas9 because it targets DNA and is the most popular and thoroughly studied Cas system to date.
